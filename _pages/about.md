I’m [Paul D. Martin, Ph.D.](https://pauldmartin.phd), and Technicomp Labs is my independent computing laboratory. I build machines, measure their limits, modify hardware and software, and restore older systems to working condition. Across current systems and computing history, I want to understand how a machine works well enough to improve it and keep it useful.

My largest current project is [Technicomp Benchtop Linux](/benchtop/), a stabilized workstation rolling release derived from openSUSE Aeon and built on Tumbleweed. The system arrives with its workstation tools and integration in place, while applications and project environments remain under the user’s control. It is currently in alpha, and its source, packaging, and design notes are public.

The [LLM Performance Engineering Notebook](https://github.com/pauldmartinphd/llm-performance-engineering-notebook) records performance experiments on my own hardware, beginning with the inference limits of large Mixture-of-Experts models. It includes the measurements, patches, and hypotheses that testing did not support, so readers can follow how I reached a conclusion and where the evidence stops.

I use the same bench to maintain [the collection](/collection/): high-end computers and game consoles from the late 1970s through the 2000s. Each machine is restored and then upgraded to the limits of its era, bringing the performance work and the restoration work together around an interest in what the hardware can do.

[The blog](/blog/) collects my write-ups on operating systems, performance and benchmarking, applied machine learning, security, hardware and firmware, and restoration. Where a conclusion depends on performance or behavior, I use measurements from [the lab](/lab/) to explain it.

Technicomp Labs is my personal, independent work, separate from my [professional research and expert witness practice](https://pauldmartin.phd).
