I’m Paul D. Martin, Ph.D. I’m a Lecturer in Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University, where I also earned my B.S., M.S.E., and Ph.D. in Computer Science. My doctoral research, advised by [Aviel D. Rubin](https://avirubin.com), focused on securing medical devices and protecting patient privacy. I’m a named inventor on five U.S. patents, Secretary of the [IEEE 7024 Working Group](https://development.standards.ieee.org/myproject-web/public/view.html#pardetail/13860), and a former member of the program committee for the IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy. My professional work is described at [pauldmartin.phd](https://pauldmartin.phd).

Technicomp Labs is separate from that work. It is my independent computing laboratory, a personal and noncommercial project that follows my own interests.

My interests fall into four areas. The first is computer history and retro technology, including retro gaming. I collect, restore, and upgrade high-end computers and game consoles from the late 1970s through the 2000s, and I like to find out how far each machine can be taken. The second is systems: how they are built, virtualized, measured, and tuned, which covers [Benchtop Linux](/benchtop/) and the experiments in the [lab notebook](https://github.com/pauldmartinphd/llm-performance-engineering-notebook). The third is open source, tech sovereignty, and self-hosting, meaning the ability to run and control your own computing. I run a homelab, and I release the software I write openly where licensing allows. The fourth is technology education, from childhood through adulthood: how people of every age learn to understand and work with computers, which is a large part of why I teach.

The [blog](/blog/) covers restoring and upgrading vintage computers to their maximum performance, particularly high-end systems, along with write-ups on virtualization, self-hosting, operating systems, and hardware and firmware. The [projects](/projects/) are my open-source work. [Benchtop Linux](/benchtop/) is the largest: a stabilized workstation rolling release derived from openSUSE Aeon and built on Tumbleweed, currently in alpha, with its source, packaging, and design notes public. I use measurements from [the lab](/lab/) to support what I write, and the machines I restore are in [the collection](/collection/).
