Technicomp Labs is the independent computing laboratory of [Paul D. Martin, Ph.D.](https://pauldmartin.phd) Its work spans current systems and computing history: building machines, measuring their limits, modifying hardware and software, and restoring older systems to working condition. The common thread is an interest in understanding computers closely enough to improve them, measure them, and keep them useful.

The largest current project is [Technicomp Benchtop Linux](/benchtop/), a stabilized workstation rolling release derived from openSUSE Tumbleweed and MicroOS. Benchtop treats the operating system as a complete workstation platform rather than a base installation for the user to assemble. It is currently in alpha, and its source, packaging, and design notes are public.

The lab also publishes performance research measured on its own hardware. The [LLM Performance Engineering Notebook](https://github.com/pauldmartinphd/llm-performance-engineering-notebook) documents experiments on the inference speed limits of large Mixture-of-Experts models, including the patches that came out of them and the hypotheses that testing did not support. The same bench serves [the collection](/collection/), a set of high-end computers and game consoles from the late 1970s through the 2000s, each restored and then upgraded to the limits of its era.

[The blog](/blog/) collects write-ups from all of this work: operating systems, performance and benchmarking, applied machine learning, security, hardware and firmware, and restoration. Posts rely on measurements from [the lab](/lab/) where measurements apply.

Technicomp Labs is personal, independent work, separate from Dr. Martin's [professional research and expert witness practice](https://pauldmartin.phd).
