I’m Paul D. Martin, Ph.D. I’m a Lecturer in Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University, where I also earned my B.S., M.S.E., and Ph.D. in Computer Science. My doctoral research, advised by [Aviel D. Rubin](https://avirubin.com), focused on securing medical devices and protecting patient privacy. I’m a named inventor on five U.S. patents, Secretary of the [IEEE 7024 Working Group](https://development.standards.ieee.org/myproject-web/public/view.html#pardetail/13860), and a former member of the program committee for the IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy. My professional work is described at [pauldmartin.phd](https://pauldmartin.phd).

Technicomp Labs is separate from that work. It is my independent computing laboratory, a personal and noncommercial project that follows my own interests. I build machines, measure their limits, modify hardware and software, and restore older systems to working condition, because I want to understand how a machine works well enough to improve it and keep it useful.

The site has two main purposes. The [blog](/blog/) covers restoring and upgrading vintage computers to their maximum performance, particularly high-end systems, along with write-ups on operating systems, benchmarking, security, and hardware and firmware. The [projects](/projects/) are the open-source work I release, and I use measurements from [the lab](/lab/) to support what I write about them.

My largest project is [Technicomp Benchtop Linux](/benchtop/), a stabilized workstation rolling release derived from openSUSE Aeon and built on Tumbleweed. It is in alpha, and its source, packaging, and design notes are public. The [LLM Performance Engineering Notebook](https://github.com/pauldmartinphd/llm-performance-engineering-notebook) records performance experiments on my own hardware, including the patches that came out of them and the hypotheses that testing did not support. [The collection](/collection/) holds the high-end computers and game consoles from the late 1970s through the 2000s that I restore and upgrade to the limits of their era.
